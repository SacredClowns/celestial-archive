# Source Record: Holtberg (2011)

**Full citation:** Anthony Holtberg, "Enochian Angel Magic: From John Dee to the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn," MA dissertation, University of Exeter, September 2011.

**Type:** Modern scholarly (MA dissertation)
**Badge level:** ◇
**Pages:** 93 (83 text + 10 appendix with 13 figures)
**Location in project:** `/sessions/stoic-nifty-brown/mnt/uploads/Enochian_Angel_Magic_From_John_Dee_to_th.pdf`

## Scope
Traces the transmission of Dee's angel magic from its philosophical and practical sources through Rosicrucianism, fringe-Masonry, and the English occult revival to the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and its successor orders.

## Chapter Structure
1. **The Philosophy and Practice of John Dee's Angel Magic** (pp. 9–40) — Ficino/Pico/Reuchlin as philosophical sources; grimoire traditions as practical sources; the Sigillum, Great Table, and Claves Angelicae
2. **Rosicrucianism and Dee's Hermetic Philosophy** (pp. 41–63) — Monas Hieroglyphica in Rosicrucian manifestos; Ashmole; Sibly; Barrett; Hockley
3. **The Golden Dawn of Enochian Magic in the Modern Era** (pp. 64–80) — Book H; Enochian Chess; spiritual alchemy; Felkin and Stella Matutina; Regardie's publication; Crowley's Vision and the Voice
4. **Conclusion** (pp. 81–83)

## Strengths
- Comprehensive bibliography drawing on *DGWE*, primary Peterson editions, Howe, Gilbert, Churton, and Harkness
- Clear chronological framework with specific dates for manuscript transmission
- Corrects Harkness's error on the sequence of angelic revelations
- Introduces addressative/non-addressative magic distinction (Weill-Parot)

## Limitations
- MA-level work (not peer-reviewed journal or monograph)
- Relies heavily on secondary sources for Rosicrucian transmission chain
- Chapter 3 (Golden Dawn) is shorter and less detailed than Chapters 1–2
- Does not treat the Enochian language or Calls in linguistic detail
