# Source Record: James (1984/1998)

**Full citation:** Geoffrey James, *The Enochian Magick of Dr. John Dee: The Most Powerful System of Magick in its Original, Unexpurgated Form* (St. Paul, MN: Llewellyn, 1998). Originally published as *The Enochian Evocation of Dr. John Dee* (Gillette, NJ: Heptangle Books, 1984).

**Type:** Modern edition of primary manuscripts with commentary
**Badge level:** ◇ (edition/commentary) referencing ◆ (primary manuscript texts)
**Pages:** ~210 (xxvii preface + 181 main text + appendices)
**Location in project:** `/sessions/stoic-nifty-brown/geoffrey_james_enochian.pdf`

## Scope

Presents the Enochian system in its original Dee manuscript form — before Golden Dawn modifications — organized into five books covering the cosmological framework, the Mystical Heptarchy, all 48 Angelic Keys with word-by-word analysis, the 30 Aethyrs with their governors, and the Watchtower angel hierarchies. Includes James's analytical preface addressing Kelly's authorship, Gnostic parallels, and the nature of the Angelical language.

## Book Structure

1. **Preface** (pp. xiv–xxvii) — James's analysis of Dee and Kelly; Kelly authorship question; Gnostic parallels; language characteristics; dictation evidence; the wife-swapping incident
2. **Book One: The Magick of Enoch** (pp. 1–16, 6 chapters) — The cosmological narrative as told by the angels: Fall of Man, Wisdom of Enoch, Fall of Enoch's Magick, Reestablishment through Dee, Nature of the Wisdom, General Considerations
3. **Book Two: The Mystical Heptarchy** (pp. 17–64, 7 chapters) — The septenary system of planetary kings and princes, with their offices, invocations, and sigils for each day of the week
4. **Book Three: The Forty-Eight Angelic Keys** (pp. 65–102) — All 19 Calls (numbered as 48 "keys" because the 19th Call generates 30 variants for each Aethyr) presented word-by-word with: line number, Enochian word, pronunciation, English translation, and extensive textual-critical footnotes
5. **Book Four: Earthly Knowledge, Aid & Victory** (pp. 103–115) — The 30 Aethyrs with their 91 governors, sigils, associated regions, and numerical values; organized by Order (LIL through TEX)
6. **Book Five: The Angels of the Four Quarters** (pp. 117–143) — The Great Table (original and 1587 corrected version), Great Circle of the Quarters, Fundamental Obeisance, Twenty-Four Seniors, Angels of Medicine, Angels of Precious Stones, Angels of Transformation — all with invocation templates and tablet diagrams

## Manuscript Sources Used by James

- Magical sigils reproduced by permission of the British Library

## Strengths

- **Presents the system as Dee received it** — before any Golden Dawn modifications, making it the closest available published edition to the primary manuscripts for non-specialists
- **Word-by-word Call analysis** — Each of the 19 Calls broken into numbered word entries with Enochian, pronunciation, and English; invaluable for language analysis
- **Extensive textual-critical footnotes** — Notes copying errors in Sloane MS 3191, identifies lacunae, cross-references word appearances across all Calls, proposes corrections from Royal Appendix (Cotton XLVI)
- **Gnostic parallel analysis** — Identifies specific textual parallels between the Angelic Keys and Gnostic texts (Pistis Sophia, Book of the Great Logos, Books of Ieou)
- **Linguistic analysis of Angelical** — Addresses whether the language was designed for non-human speakers (no onomatopoeia, consonant contractions, incomprehensible number system)
- **Corrected Great Table** — Presents both the original 1583 Great Table and the 1587 Raphael-corrected version
- **Complete angelic hierarchy** — Full names for all four quarters: Seniors, Angels of Medicine, Angels of Precious Stones, Angels of Transformation, with God-names and cacodemons

## Limitations

- **1984 original, 1998 reprint** — Predates Peterson's comprehensive digital editions and some modern scholarship (Harkness 1999, Asprem 2012)
- **Commentary is popular rather than academic** — James writes for a magical practitioner audience; not peer-reviewed
- **Preface makes some strong interpretive claims** — e.g., James leans toward Kelly as genuine (or at least extraordinary) rather than maintaining strict agnosticism
- **No Enochian script reproductions in the Keys** — Only Latin transliterations; the alphabet glyphs are not shown alongside the Call texts
- **Missing some manuscript variants** — Does not systematically collate all known manuscript versions the way Peterson later would
- **Number system left unexplained** — Notes the problem but offers no analysis beyond Laycock's observation
