## I. DISSERTATION THESIS

Holtberg argues that Dee's angel magic descends from two streams — Hermetic philosophy (Ficino, Pico, the *Corpus Hermeticum*) and Solomonic practical magic (grimoires like *Liber Juratus*, *Ars Notoria*, *Ars Paulina*, *Arbatel*) — and that this combined system survived Dee's posthumous disgrace through a specific chain of transmission: **Monas Hieroglyphica → Rosicrucian manifestos → Ashmole → Sibly → Barrett → Hockley → MacKenzie → Golden Dawn founders**.

The dissertation's central question: How did Dee's philosophy and practice prove resilient enough to survive Casaubon's damning *True & Faithful Relation* (1659) and persist into the modern era? ◇

## II. THE TRANSMISSION CHAIN

This is the dissertation's most valuable contribution to the Archive — a documented chain of custody for Dee's ideas across 300 years.

### Phase 1: Dee's Philosophical Sources (pre-1583)

| Figure | Dates | Contribution to Dee | Badge |
|--------|-------|-------------------|-------|
| Marsilio Ficino | 1433–1499 | Translated *Corpus Hermeticum*; developed Neoplatonic "daemonic" magic via stellar influences; concept of *prisca theologia* | ◇ |
| Giovanni Pico della Mirandola | 1463–1494 | Reconciled Kabbalah with Platonic and Hermetic philosophy; empowered the use of divine names; influenced via Agrippa | ◇ |
| Johannes Reuchlin | 1455–1522 | Deepened Kabbalah-Christianity connection; *De Verbo Mirifico* (1494) and *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517); both in Dee's catalogue | ◇ |
| Ramón Llull | 1232–1316 | Combinatorial letter-systems (*Ars Combinatoria*); mathematical-mystical diagrams; influenced Dee's systematic approach | ◇ |
| Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa | 1486–1535 | *De Occulta Philosophia* (1533) — transmitted combined Hermetic-Kabbalistic-Platonic ideas; Dee's threefold world (elementary, celestial, supercelestial) drawn from this | ◇ |
| Guillaume de Postel | 1510–1581 | Met Dee in France 1551; translator of the Zohar; seeker of *lingua adamica*; may have discussed *Book of Enoch* with Dee | ◇ |

**Key insight:** Dee's angel magic was not an abandonment of his scholarly career but "the very application of it in a context of divine madness." ◇ His angelic conversations were the culmination of his Hermetic-Kabbalistic-Neoplatonic philosophy, not a departure from it.

### Phase 1b: Dee's Practical Magic Sources

| Text | Date | Connection to Dee | Badge |
|------|------|------------------|-------|
| *Ars Almadel* | Medieval | Device for direct angelic contact; traceable to Persia/Far East; Christianized versions circulated | ◇ |
| *Liber Juratus Honorii* | Medieval | Contains earliest version of *Sigillum Dei Aemeth*; Dee owned a copy (Sloane MS 313) | ◆ |
| *Ars Notoria* | Medieval | Orations resembling *Liber Juratus*; mix of Latin with Greek, Hebrew, Arabic words perceived as Adamic language; direct precedent for Enochian language claims | ◇ |
| *Ars Paulina* | Medieval | Crystal-on-table practice; "Conjuration of the Holy Guardian Angel"; closest structural parallel to Dee's method | ◇ |
| *Arbatel de Magia Veterum* | 1575 | Overtly Christian tone; septenary structure (7×7 aphorisms); Olympic spirits paralleling Dee's angelic hierarchy | ◇ |
| Trithemius, *Steganographia* | c. 1499 | Cryptographic angel magic; spirits from second book appear in *Ars Paulina*; Dee owned/studied this | ◇ |

**Key insight:** Peterson argues Dee's methods are "a face-lift of methods well-known by other Renaissance occultists." Szönyi counters that this neglects the philosophical depth Dee drew from the "high magicians." Holtberg reconciles both views. ◇ ?

### Phase 2: The Rosicrucian Bridge (1608–1700s)

| Figure / Event | Date | Role in Transmission | Badge |
|---------------|------|---------------------|-------|
| *Monas Hieroglyphica* on *Chemical Wedding* invitation | 1616 | Dee's symbol appeared in Andreae's Rosicrucian text, linking Dee to the movement | ◆ |
| Heinrich Khunrath | 1560–1605 | Met Dee in Bremen, June 27, 1589; used Dee's *Hieroglyphic Monad* in *Amphitheatrum* (1595); bridge between Dee and Andreae's circle | ◇ |
| Johann Valentin Andreae | 1586–1654 | Authored/co-authored Rosicrucian manifestos; used Dee's symbol; connected to Dee via Khunrath and Arndt | ◇ |
| Adam Haslmayr | c. 1560–1630 | First public responder to *Fama*; reproduced *Hieroglyphic Monad* in multiple manuscripts; may have transmitted it into *Confessio* | ◇ |
| *Secretioris Philosophiae Consideratio Brevis* | 1615 | Published with *Confessio*; quotes first 13 Theorems of *Monas*; credited to pseudonymous "Phillipus a Gabella" — no mention of Dee | ◇ |
| Robert Fludd | 1574–1637 | Brought Rosicrucianism to England; defended the Fraternity; shared Dee's vision of hidden Book of Nature | ◇ |
| Elias Ashmole | 1617–1692 | Received Dee's manuscripts August 20, 1672 from Thomas Wale (found in chest that once belonged to Dee); collected, transcribed, preserved them; bequeathed to Oxford | ◆ |
| Thomas Vaughan | 1612–1666 | Royal Society alchemist; translated *Fama* and *Confessio* as *Magia Adamica*; Ashmole copied from Vaughan's manuscript | ◇ |

**Key insight on Monas plagiarism:** The *Monas Hieroglyphica* was so highly regarded that it was repeatedly plagiarized: Gerard Dorn (1568), Cesare della Riviera (1605), Athanasius Kircher (1653–1655), Johann Christoph Steeb (1679), and the *Consideratio Brevis* (1615). ◇

### Phase 3: The English Occult Revival (1750s–1880s)

| Figure | Dates | Role | Badge |
|--------|-------|------|-------|
| Ebenezer Sibly | 1751–1799 | Rejuvenated interest in Rosicrucianism in English occultism; *Complete Illustration of the Celestial Art of Astrology* (4 vols, 1784–1792); Swedenborgian worldview with angelic lamens | ◇ |
| Francis Barrett | 1774–c.1830 | *The Magus* (1801) — plagiarized Agrippa, pseudo-Agrippa, Abano; included *The Art of Drawing Spirits into Crystals* (attributed to Trithemius); connected crystal magic to Dee's tradition | ◇ |
| Frederick Hockley | 1809–1885 | Crystal scryer; collected and copied esoteric manuscripts; connected to both Barrett (via Denley) and Sibly's manuscripts; believed crystal scrying originated with Jews (Urim and Thummim) | ◇ |
| Kenneth MacKenzie | 1833–1886 | Called Hockley "the most profound Occult student in the country"; met Eliphas Lévi in Paris 1861; expert on Rosicrucian fringe-Masonry; member of Society of Eight with Hockley and Mathers | ◇ |
| William Wynn Westcott | 1848–1925 | Distrusted by Hockley/MacKenzie/Irwin; may have created Golden Dawn partly in response to being refused entry to Society of Eight; praised Hockley and MacKenzie in his Historical Lecture | ◇ ? |

**Manuscript transmission chain:**
Dee's manuscripts → found in chest by Thomas Wale's wife → delivered to Ashmole (Aug 20, 1672) ◆ → bequeathed to Oxford → portions transcribed → influenced Sibly → Sibly's library → bookseller John Denley → Barrett → Barrett's acquaintance Hockley → Hockley lent freely to MacKenzie → MacKenzie and Mathers in Society of Eight → Westcott, Mathers, and Woodman found Golden Dawn (1888) ◇

| Figure / Event | Date | Role | Badge |
|---------------|------|------|-------|

## III. DEE'S PRACTICE — KEY STRUCTURAL DETAILS

### Chronology of Angelic Revelations (from Holtberg)

| Date | Material Received | Source MS | Badge |
|------|------------------|-----------|-------|
| March 10–15, 1582 | Holy Table and ritual implements | *Five Books of Mystery*, pp. 61–73 | ◆ |
| March 19–21, 1582 | *Sigillum Dei Aemeth* | *Five Books of Mystery*, pp. 89–148 | ◆ |
| April 29 – November 21, 1582 | *Tabula Angelorum Bonum 49* → *De Heptarchia Mystica* | *Five Books of Mystery*, pp. 183–254 | ◆ |
| March 23 – May 6, 1583 | *Liber Loagaeth* and Great Table | *TFR*, pp. 82–210 | ◆ |
| March 24, 1583 | *Liber Loagaeth* delivery begins | *Five Books of Mystery*, pp. 261–71 | ◆ |
| April 14 – July 13, 1584 | *48 Claves Angelicae* (the Angelic Keys) | *TFR*, pp. 268–71 | ◆ |
| May 21–23, 1584 | *Liber Scientiae Auxilii* (nations listed) | *TFR*, pp. 138–159 | ◆ |
| June 20, 1584 | *Tabula Bonorum Angelorum Invocationes* begins (24 Seniors mentioned) | *TFR*, pp. 170–85 | ◆ |

**Critical correction to common misconception:** Harkness incorrectly states the first book received was *Liber Loagaeth*. Holtberg clarifies that the *De Heptarchia Mystica* source material was received first (April 29, 1582), and *Liber Loagaeth* delivery did not begin until March 24, 1583. ◇

### The Sigillum Dei Aemeth

- Created using names from the septenary of septenary angels ◆
- One source: Jewish planetary angel names with Hebrew "El" suffix (Zabathiel, Zedekiel, Madimiel, Semeliel, Nogahel, Corabiel, Leuanael) ◆
- Second source: Agrippa's "Scale of the Number Seven" (Zaphkiel, Zadkiel, Cumael, Raphael, Haniel, Michael, Gabriel) — also correspond with Geonic lore and Pseudo-Dionysius ◇
- Names encoded via 7×7 letter squares read vertically ◆
- Central heptagram: seven angels I, Ih, Ilr, Dmal, Heeoa, Beigia, Stimcul (Sons of Light) ◆
- Innermost heptagon: Il, An, Ave, Liba, Rocle, Hagonel, Ilemese (Sons of the Sons of Light) ◆

### The Great Table

- 25×27 table containing 664 letters ◇
- Central cross of letters contains eleven blank spaces ◇
- Divided into four quadrants (cardinal directions) with six Seniors each ◆
- Each quadrant subdivided into four sub-quadrants by internal crosses ◆
- Sub-quadrant letters generate names of angels of medicine, precious stones, transformation, four elements, natural substances, transportation, mechanical arts, and secret discovery ◆
- Three "mystical names" (banners) per direction drawn from horizontal cross lines ◆
- The Golden Dawn renamed the four quadrants "Elemental Tablets" or "Watchtowers" △

### Addressative vs. Non-Addressative Magic

Holtberg introduces Weill-Parot's distinction: Dee's magic was "addressative" — focused on the relationship between spirit and magician, not on the inherent power of magical objects. ◇ The Golden Dawn shifted toward "non-addressative" magic — using talismans and images as psychological/alchemical focuses for inner transmutation. ◇

### The 49 Gates of Understanding

- The *Claves Angelicae* (19 Keys) open 49 (or 48 + 1 forbidden) Gates of Understanding ◆
- The 49 Gates = secrets of the word of God (the Torah) in Kabbalistic tradition ◇
- Connected to *Shemittah* cycles: 49,000 years = 7 cosmic Sabbaths; 50th = cosmic Yovel (Jubilee) ◇
- Dee's apocalyptic purpose: through the Keys, restore the holy books lost since the beginning, decipher true religion from false, THEN COMES THE END ◆

## IV. KEY SCHOLARLY CLAIMS AND DISPUTES

| Claim | Source | Counter | Badge |
|-------|--------|---------|-------|
| Dee's methods are just a "face-lift" of existing grimoire practices | Peterson ◇ | Szönyi: this neglects the philosophical depth from Ficino/Pico/Agrippa | ? |
| Enochian has more in common with Ethiopic script than proto-Semitic or Egyptian | Laycock ◇ | Suggests Dee may have had access to an Ethiopian manuscript he couldn't read | ~ |
| Casaubon's *TFR* (1659) sealed Dee's reputation as a "deluded diabolist" | Holtberg/French ◇ | Ashmole and Rosicrucians preserved a counter-narrative via the *Monas* | ◇ |
| The separation of magic from religion has "clouded" scholarly understanding of Dee | Holtberg's own argument ◇ | Treats magic and religion as synonyms for Dee's purposes | ◇ |
| Westcott created the Golden Dawn partly in response to being excluded from the Society of Eight | Hamill ◇ | Speculative connection; documented that Hockley, MacKenzie, and Irwin distrusted Westcott | ~ |
| Dee's *Liber Loagaeth* was the first book received from the angels | Harkness ◇ | Holtberg corrects: *De Heptarchia Mystica* material received first (April 1582 vs. March 1583) | ◇ |
| The Golden Dawn's Enochian system greatly expanded beyond Dee's original work | Holtberg ◇ | Westcott and Mathers "saw the potential for correspondences" and enriched rituals/practices | ◇ |
