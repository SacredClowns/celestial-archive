## I. INTRODUCTION TEXT

### Landing Page — Primary Introduction

On March 26, 1583, Edward Kelley reported seeing a grid of 21 letters in the scrying stone — each with a name, a shape, and a sound. ◆ John Dee recorded them carefully, mapping each to its nearest English equivalent, noting the script ran right to left. ◆ Over the next year, through these letters, an entire language would emerge: vocabulary, grammar fragments, and 19 invocatory texts that Dee called the Angelic Keys.

What you are entering is the Language Chamber — the Archive's instrument for hearing, seeing, and studying what was received. The alphabet is here, with three different traditions for how each letter sounds. A dictionary of approximately 250 attested words is here, drawn from the Calls and Dee's journals. ◇ The 19 Calls themselves are here, line by line, word by word, with Dee's English translations beside them.

Whether this constitutes a natural language, a constructed language, or something else entirely is debated. ◇ The Chamber does not settle that question. It makes the material available so you can listen for yourself.

### Writing Direction Note

The Enochian script is written from right to left, as recorded in Dee's manuscripts. ◆ This direction is consistent across all manuscript sources, though modern practice sometimes renders the text left to right in Latin transliteration. Different manuscript sources show slightly different forms of the letter glyphs. ◇

### Font Note

Several digital fonts reproduce the Enochian script. They typically map each glyph to the ASCII codepoint of its English equivalent — so typing "A" in an Enochian font renders the glyph for Un. ◇

## III. PRONUNCIATION GUIDE

### Section Header

How did Enochian sound? Three traditions offer different answers. All are presented here — none is ranked as "correct."

### Tradition 1: Dee's Original (Reconstructed)

Dee wrote pronunciation notes alongside the Angelic Calls — marking stressed vowels, indicating soft consonants with specific notation (e.g., "dg" for soft g), and occasionally giving English rhymes (most famously: "zorge" is said to rhyme with "George"). ◆ Combined with what historical linguists know about Elizabethan English, Laycock produced a phonological reconstruction that makes Enochian sound, as he put it, "thoroughly English." ◇

This is the most historically grounded pronunciation. It is also the least mystical-sounding — which, depending on your perspective, is either a feature or a limitation.

### Tradition 2: Golden Dawn Syllabic

Wynn Westcott and the Golden Dawn developed a method where each consonant is pronounced as a full syllable, inserting a vowel sound drawn from the Hebrew name of the corresponding letter. △ For example, N is not just [n] but may be pronounced [ɛn] or [nuː] (from Hebrew *nun*). Consonant-vowel sequences can optionally be merged into single syllables — so "ta" might be pronounced [teɪ ɑː] or collapsed to [tɑː]. △

This method produces a more fluid, chant-like sound. It diverges significantly from Dee's own pronunciation notes, but has been the dominant method in Western ceremonial magic for over a century. △

### Tradition 3: Modern Phonetic (DuQuette)

Lon Milo DuQuette and many contemporary practitioners simply sound out the words as written, inserting natural vowel sounds where clusters of consonants make pronunciation difficult. ○ DuQuette recommends this as the most accessible method for beginners. ○

This is the simplest approach. It has no historical or traditional authority beyond practical accessibility.

### Comparison Note

The three traditions agree on vowels and most consonants. They diverge primarily on how consonant clusters are handled and whether each consonant carries its own syllable. For the reader who wants to recite the Calls, the choice of tradition is a personal one — the Archive presents all three without preference.

## V. THE 19 CALLS

### Section Header

The Angelic Calls — also known as the Keys or *Claves Angelicae* — were received in 1584 at the court of King Stephen Báthory in Kraków. ◆ Dictated through Kelley in reverse order (19th first, 1st last), they were understood as verbal keys that open specific regions of the Enochian cosmology. ◆

Dee recorded both the Enochian text and English translations. ◆ What follows is the text as Dee received it, with word-by-word dictionary references and pronunciation guides in three traditions.
