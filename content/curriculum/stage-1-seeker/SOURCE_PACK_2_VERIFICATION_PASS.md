## Item 1 — Sloane MS 3188, Folio Location for 10 March 1582 Session

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 flagged *"folio numbers for the 10 March 1582 session"* as requiring confirmation against either the Peterson 2003 folio concordance or the British Library catalogue.

### Strongest currently available source

- **Peterson, *John Dee's Five Books of Mystery* (2003)** — the standard critical edition, which supplies a full folio concordance.
- **Peterson's *Esoteric Archives* digital edition** (esotericarchives.com) — open-access transcription that mirrors the manuscript's internal structure.
- **British Library catalogue record for Sloane MS 3188** — authoritative repository metadata.

### Verdict

**Status:** Partially Confirmed.

The manuscript structure is confirmed in published sources: *Mysteriorum Liber Primus* runs from **22 December 1581 to 14 March 1582** and occupies **folios 1r–15v** of Sloane MS 3188. The 10 March 1582 session — Kelley's third recorded sitting — therefore falls within this range, in the final quarter of *Liber Primus*.

The **exact folio** for the 10 March session (likely fol. 13r–14v, but the precise leaf count depends on Dee's session lengths in the immediately preceding entries) cannot be fixed from secondary literature alone. It requires either a direct consultation of Peterson's 2003 concordance page or of the British Library digital surrogate.

### Confidence

**High** for the folio *range* (ff. 1–15, *Liber Primus*).
**Low** for the *exact folio* of 10 March 1582.

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> Sloane MS 3188, *Mysteriorum Liber Primus* (fols. 1–15), British Library. The first months of the Mortlake sessions, December 1581 through mid-March 1582.

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** print a specific folio (e.g., "fol. 13r") without confirming against Peterson 2003.
- Do **not** imply that session dates map one-to-one onto folio numbers — Dee's session entries vary in length.

### Rank discipline

- **S1–S2:** Quote the range *Liber Primus*, fols. 1–15.
- **S3+:** Quote the exact folio once Peterson 2003 is in hand.

## Item 2 — Ashmole MSS Shelfmarks for Kelley's Biographical Material

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 named Ashmole MS 1790, 1789, and 580 as the probable loci of Ashmole's Kelley biographical notes. This was a best-guess constellation based on general Ashmole-Dee associations, not on a verified provenance chain.

### Strongest currently available sources

- **Bodleian Library Ashmole Catalogue (Black, 1845; and Digital Bodleian)** — the definitive shelfmark directory.
- **Sidney Lee (ed.), *Dictionary of National Biography* (1885–1900), entry for "Kelley, Edward"** — references Ashmole's published and manuscript material directly. (Wikisource preserves the text.)
- **Ashmole, Elias, *Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum* (London, 1652), p. 479 and surrounding commentary** — Ashmole's *printed* biographical note on Kelley, which includes the horoscope Dee cast for Kelley and much of the story that later biographers repeat.

### Verdict

**Status:** Corrected.

The original Source Pack 2 citation was structurally mistaken in an important way. The "cropped ears at Lancaster for forging title-deeds" story does **not** originate in Ashmole's *unpublished* manuscripts. It is transmitted chiefly through:

1. **Ashmole, *Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum* (1652)** — the earliest printed appearance within the "Dee-Kelley antiquarian" tradition, including the Kelley horoscope.
2. **Anthony Wood, *Athenae Oxonienses* (1691–92)** and **Thomas Fuller, *History of the Worthies of England* (1662)** — 17th-century biographical compilations that repeat and amplify the story.
3. **Ashmole MS 1790 (Bodleian)** — contains Dee-Kelley spirit-action material (Dee's Latin copy of additional actions); it is a Dee/Kelley *manuscript* source, not Ashmole's biographical note on Kelley.

The correct statement is therefore that **Ashmole is the earliest printed conduit of the biographical tradition (via *Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum*, 1652)**, and that the *unpublished* manuscript material in MS Ashmole 1790 preserves the Dee-Kelley spirit records rather than Ashmole's editorial biographical commentary. The "cropped ears" and "forgery" stories are transmitted through the printed antiquarian tradition (Ashmole 1652 → Wood 1691 → Fuller 1662 → 18th/19th c. biographies → DNB 1885–1900), not through a single manuscript shelfmark.

### Confidence

**High** for the corrected attribution chain.
**Moderate** for the specific Bodleian shelfmark of the *Theatrum Chemicum* commentary notes (MS Ashmole 1790 is confirmed as Dee/Kelley material; the broader biographical content attributed to Ashmole's notes in Victorian-era scholarship requires Bodleian catalogue confirmation before specific shelfmarks beyond 1790 are printed).

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> The biographical tradition surrounding Kelley's early life — including the cropped-ears and forgery stories — is transmitted chiefly through Elias Ashmole's *Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum* (London, 1652) and the antiquarian biography of Anthony Wood (*Athenae Oxonienses*, 1691–92) and Thomas Fuller (*Worthies of England*, 1662). Ashmole's Dee/Kelley manuscript materials are preserved in the Bodleian Library (including MS Ashmole 1790). No contemporary court or session record of the cropped-ears punishment has been identified in the surviving Elizabethan archives.

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** state or imply that MS Ashmole 1790 contains the "cropped ears" biography. It does not.
- Do **not** present Ashmole 1789 or Ashmole 580 as confirmed loci for the Kelley biographical material — these were speculative shelfmarks in the prior pack and are withdrawn pending direct Bodleian confirmation.
- Do **not** conflate Ashmole's *printed* commentary (1652) with his *manuscript* notes.

### Rank discipline

- **S1–S2:** The printed tradition (Ashmole 1652; Wood 1691; Fuller 1662) is the citation. Do not cite manuscripts at this level.
- **S3+:** Add MS Ashmole 1790 as the Dee-Kelley session material.
- **S6+:** Only after a direct Bodleian catalogue check may the Archive cite specific Ashmole shelfmarks for the *biographical* content beyond the printed 1652 source.

## Item 3 — Casaubon 1659, Page Range for the 1587 Incident

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 tentatively placed the 1587 cross-matching incident in Casaubon 1659 *"in the range of pp. 20–25 of the main body, c. April 1587 sessions"* and flagged this for facsimile verification.

### Strongest currently available sources

- **Meric Casaubon, *A True & Faithful Relation of What Passed for Many Yeers Between Dr. John Dee and Some Spirits* (London: Maxwell for Garthwait, 1659)** — the only primary source in printed form.
- **Peterson's *Esoteric Archives* digital excerpt** (esotericarchives.com/dee/tfr/tfr.htm).
- **Cotton Appendix XLVI** — the underlying manuscript; the Casaubon text is a selection from these spirit diaries.

### Verdict

**Status:** Unresolved.

The 1659 folio edition is paginated **pp. 1–256, 353–448** (with an unpaginated preface and a gap in the numbering; these two page runs are the main body of the diary selections). The April–May 1587 incident belongs to the Trebon / Bohemian portion of the continental sessions — material that falls in the **later** of the two paginated blocks (pp. 353–448), not in the first block and not in the Seeker-rank range originally posited.

A specific page anchor cannot be responsibly printed without either (a) a facsimile of the 1659 folio in hand, or (b) a citation from a modern scholarly work that quotes the page directly. Neither was confirmable from the available open sources during this pass.

### Confidence

**Low** for exact page anchor.
**Moderate** for the *block* within which the incident sits (pp. 353–448).

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> Meric Casaubon, *A True & Faithful Relation of What Passed for Many Yeers Between Dr. John Dee and Some Spirits* (London, 1659), in the later continental session material. Casaubon's printing draws from the manuscripts now held as Cotton Appendix XLVI, British Library.

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** print a specific page number (e.g., "p. 21") or range (e.g., "pp. 20–25") until verified against a 1659 folio or a modern scholarly citation.
- Do **not** quote Casaubon's text *directly* at Seeker rank — the 1587 incident's content is held back to Observer rank (§VI.5 of Source Pack 2). Paraphrase only.

### Rank discipline

- **S1–S2:** Name the source, do not cite a page, do not quote the content.
- **S3 (Observer):** The content of the incident may be presented. Citations are still paraphrased unless verified page anchors are in hand.
- **S6+:** Direct quotation with specific pagination only after facsimile verification.

## Item 4 — British Museum Registration Number for "Dee's Crystal"

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 printed **"OA.58"** as the registration number for Dee's crystal shew-stone, flagged for verification.

### Strongest currently available sources

- **British Museum online catalogue** (britishmuseum.org/collection).
- **Tait, H., et al., *"The Lord of the Smoking Mirror: Objects associated with John Dee in the British Museum"*** — *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A*, 43 (2012), the authoritative object-study.
- **The British Museum Dr John Dee object group** — six associated objects, publicly catalogued.

### Verdict

**Status:** Corrected / Partially Confirmed.

The British Museum's "John Dee" object group consists of **six confirmed items**: three wax discs (Sigillum and smaller seals), one obsidian mirror, one gold disc, and one crystal ball. The public catalogue numbers that I can positively confirm are:

| Object | Confirmed registration |
|:---|:---|
| Obsidian mirror (with case) | **1966,1001.1** |
| Gold disc ("Vision of the Four Castles") | **1942,0506.1** |
| Wax discs (Sigillum Dei Aemeth + smaller supporting seals) | **1838,1232.90** with letter suffixes (.a, .b, and additional letters) |
| Crystal ball ("shew-stone") | Catalogued within the John Dee group, c. 5.2 cm diameter rock crystal; **"OA.58" is not verifiable in the current British Museum online catalogue**. The Tait 2012 study is the definitive source for the modern registration, which should be confirmed against britishmuseum.org before the Archive prints a specific number. |

The originally posited **OA.58** must be **withdrawn** as unverified. The "OA" prefix (older Oriental Antiquities / Anthropological accession series) is plausible for older British Museum numbering, but no publicly retrievable object under that specific number corresponds to the crystal shew-stone.

### Confidence

**High** for the existence and general provenance of the crystal in the British Museum John Dee group.
**Low** for the specific registration number. OA.58 is withdrawn.

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> A small rock crystal sphere (c. 5.2 cm diameter), held within the British Museum's group of objects associated with John Dee. The crystal is traditionally identified as one of Dee's shew-stones; its provenance, unlike the obsidian mirror's, is not fully documented from Dee's lifetime forward. Catalogued alongside the obsidian mirror (1966,1001.1), the gold disc (1942,0506.1), and the wax seals (1838,1232.90, series).

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** print "OA.58" as the registration number.
- Do **not** claim an unbroken provenance from Dee's hand to the British Museum — the documentary chain has gaps.
- Do **not** describe the crystal as *the* principal shew-stone used in the sessions; it is *a* crystal associated with Dee, and the sessions used multiple stones over their course.

### Rank discipline

- **S1–S2:** Describe by type, size, and group membership. Omit the specific registration number.
- **S3+:** Once the current British Museum catalogue number is confirmed (single online lookup), it may be printed alongside the Tait 2012 citation.

## Item 5 — British Museum Registration Numbers for the Wax Seals

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 gave **"1838,1232.90.a–e"** as the registration range for the Sigillum Dei Aemeth and the four smaller supporting seals, flagged for verification.

### Strongest currently available sources

- **British Museum online catalogue** — object records retrievable at URLs of the form `britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1838-1232-90-a`, `_1838-1232-90-b`, etc.
- **Tait et al. 2012** — the authoritative object study.

### Verdict

**Status:** Confirmed.

The base registration **1838,1232.90** with letter suffixes is confirmed against the British Museum online catalogue. At least **.a** (the Sigillum Dei Aemeth) and **.b** (a smaller magical disc) are publicly retrievable. The canonical set comprises the one large Sigillum plus four smaller discs (one for each leg of the Holy Table), so suffixes through **.e** are structurally expected and consistent with the catalogue's indexing convention — but the Archive should perform one catalogue lookup to confirm the full .a–.e range before printing it as a fact.

### Confidence

**High** for the base registration **1838,1232.90**.
**Moderate** for the full .a–.e letter range (structurally expected; direct catalogue confirmation recommended).

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> The wax seals — the large Sigillum Dei Aemeth and the four smaller supporting discs for the legs of the Holy Table — are held by the British Museum under registration 1838,1232.90 (with letter suffixes for the individual pieces). They were acquired via the Sir Hans Sloane bequest in the 18th century.

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** claim that the Holy Table itself survives — only the seals do. The table is a reconstruction in all modern settings.
- Do **not** state the exact count of surviving smaller seals as "four" without catalogue confirmation of the .b / .c / .d / .e breakdown.

### Rank discipline

- **S1 onward:** The base registration is printable. The letter-suffix breakdown is printable after one catalogue check.

## Item 6 — Rudolfine Court Records for Kelley's Employment

### The unresolved citation

Source Pack 2 called for the archival provenance of the Rudolfine (Holy Roman Imperial) court records documenting Kelley's post-1587 employment, imprisonment, and death.

### Strongest currently available sources

- **Charles Nicholl, "The Last Years of Edward Kelley, Alchemist to the Emperor"**, *London Review of Books*, vol. 23, no. 8 (19 April 2001) — the most careful modern summary of the Prague / Bohemia primary record.
- **R.J.W. Evans, *Rudolf II and His World: A Study in Intellectual History, 1576–1612*** (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1973; reprint Thames & Hudson, 1997) — the standard study of the Rudolfine court.
- **Pamela H. Smith, *The Business of Alchemy: Science and Culture in the Holy Roman Empire*** (Princeton University Press, 1994) — the standard study of court alchemy.
- Czech archival holdings (National Archives, Prague; the Lobkowicz family archive at Roudnice / Nelahozeves) for the Bohemian legal records — not directly consulted in this pass.

### Verdict

**Status:** Confirmed (for the key events and dates); the exact archival shelfmarks remain to be filled in at Observer rank+.

Key datapoints are strongly documented in published scholarship:

| Event | Date | Source in published scholarship |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Kelley arrested at the inn at Soběslav, Bohemia | **3 May 1591** | Nicholl 2001; Evans 1973 |
| Royal edict indicting Kelley as criminal and fugitive | **2 May 1591** | Nicholl 2001 |
| First imprisonment at Křivoklát Castle | from 1591 | Nicholl 2001; Evans 1973 |
| Complaint filed by Gregor Böul (Rudolf's quartermaster) detailing Kelley's custody | **22 March 1597** | Nicholl 2001 |
| Kelley's death | c. 1597–98 (date and circumstance contested; traditional account: fatal injury during an escape attempt from Hněvín / Most Castle) | Nicholl 2001; Bassnett ODNB 2008 |

### Confidence

**High** for the published framework of dates and events.
**Moderate** for the precise identity of the issuing chancery series.
**Low** for specific Czech archive shelfmarks — these require a specialist visit or a citation from the specialized Bohemian-studies literature.

### Safe public wording for the Archive (Seeker rank)

> After the rupture with Dee, Kelley continued in service at the court of Emperor Rudolf II in Prague as an alchemist. A royal edict dated 2 May 1591 moved against him; he was arrested at Soběslav on 3 May 1591 and imprisoned at Křivoklát Castle. He appears in subsequent court documents, including a 1597 complaint by the imperial quartermaster Gregor Böul. He died c. 1597–98 under circumstances still debated by historians.

### What the Archive must not imply

- Do **not** present the traditional "death during an escape attempt" as established fact — it is the dominant account but not the only one.
- Do **not** romanticize the imprisonment as a martyrdom of the magus; the court's motives were financial and political.
- Do **not** cite specific Czech archive shelfmarks (Národní archiv Praha; Státní oblastní archiv) until consulted directly.

### Rank discipline

- **S1–S2:** Summary of events, with dates from Nicholl 2001 / Evans 1973.
- **S3+:** Add the 1591 edict and 1597 Böul complaint as named documents.
- **S4+:** Archival shelfmarks (once filled in) and comparative reading alongside Evans 1973 and Smith 1994.
