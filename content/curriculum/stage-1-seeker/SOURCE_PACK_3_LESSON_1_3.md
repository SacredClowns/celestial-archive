# §III — The **◎** Parallel Badge — Formal Justification

This is the load-bearing section of this Source Pack. The **◎** badge is inducted into the Archive's canonical vocabulary here. Every future use of **◎** across the curriculum refers back to this definition.

## III.1 Formal Definition

> **◎ — Parallel.** A claim that a structural, formal, iconographic, or motific element in the Dee–Kelley material has a demonstrable precedent or counterpart in another tradition, text, or body of material, where the resemblance is specific enough to be non-trivial, but the historical relationship — whether inheritance, independent convergence, or coincidence — is **either unproven or deliberately bracketed**.
>
> The **◎** badge asserts **resemblance of form**. It does **not** assert causal descent, conscious borrowing, metaphysical identity, or archetypal necessity. A parallel is a piece of evidence about the *landscape*. It tells the reader: *this pattern exists elsewhere too. You may find the resemblance informative. The Archive is not yet telling you what the resemblance means.*

## III.2 What Qualifies as **◎**

A claim earns the **◎** badge when **all** of the following conditions are met:

1. **Specificity.** The parallel is specific, not general. "Both have angels" is not a **◎**. "Both describe a heptagonal arrangement of divine names inside two concentric circles with a pentagram at center" is a **◎**.
2. **Documentary presence.** The parallel can be pointed to in an identifiable source, with folio, page, or image reference. **◎** is never invoked from memory or intuition.
3. **Scholarly recognition.** At least one modern critical scholar has identified the parallel in print. The Archive does not manufacture its own parallels.
4. **Epistemic humility.** The Archive explicitly refrains from claiming that the parallel proves inheritance, influence, or spiritual identity.

## III.3 What Does **Not** Qualify

The following disqualify a claim from **◎**:

- Generic or structural claims so broad they would match almost any tradition ("both involve light," "both use sacred names").
- Parallels that exist only because the reader has imported them — i.e., the similarity is retrospective, based on occultist-era synthesis rather than on the documents themselves.
- Claims that require psychological or archetypal frameworks to establish the parallel (those go under **△** or **~**).
- Resemblances that rest on texts Dee demonstrably did not have access to, *unless* the Archive is explicitly flagging a trans-historical convergence as a **◎** with that qualification stated in-line (see ◎5 — the *1 Enoch* case).
- "This feels similar to X." Feeling is not a citation. The **◎** badge cannot be earned by vibe.

## III.4 Distinctions from Adjacent Badges

The **◎** badge is most easily confused with **○**, **△**, and **~**. Here is how it differs:

| Badge | Asserts | Grounded in | Characteristic move |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **○** Traditional Occult Claim | *This tradition says X.* | The internal record of an occult tradition (Golden Dawn, Thelema, etc.) | Attributes a statement to a tradition, without endorsing it |
| **△** Later Interpretation | *A later commentator read the material this way.* | Post-facto interpretive work, often from a different era or framework | Names the interpreter and the era |
| **~** Speculative | *One might argue X, but evidence is thin.* | Insufficiently supported reasoning, often interesting but unproven | Flags the argument as suggestive, not demonstrable |
| **◎** Parallel | *The same structural pattern exists elsewhere.* | Documentary evidence of resemblance, identified by modern scholarship | Points to the other source and says "notice" — without yet interpreting |

The critical distinction: **○** and **△** are about what *people said*; **~** is about what the Archive *cannot yet defend*; **◎** is about what *the materials themselves* formally resemble, regardless of whether anyone has yet claimed a connection.

## III.5 The Danger the Badge Protects Against

The most common failure mode in this field is **parallel inflation** — the assumption that structural resemblance proves:
- historical inheritance (one text borrowed from the other)
- metaphysical identity (both point to the same underlying truth)
- or archetypal necessity (the human mind inevitably produces this pattern)

All three assumptions are common, and all three are *interpretive moves*, not findings. **◎** exists precisely so the Archive can honestly mark a resemblance without being pulled into any of those interpretations against its will.

When the reader sees **◎**, they should understand: *the Archive is showing me a pattern. It is not yet telling me what the pattern means.*

## III.6 The Inaugural **◎** Claim

The **◎** badge is first deployed in Lesson 1.3 around the **Sigillum Dei Aemeth** and its medieval precedents in the *Liber Juratus* tradition (Sloane MS 313 among its witnesses). The parallel meets all four qualification conditions:

1. **Specificity:** both contain two concentric circles, a heptagonal organization of divine names, a central pentagram, and Hebrew-Latin-Greek onomastic material.
2. **Documentary presence:** Sloane MS 313 is a physical manuscript with extant images; the *Liber Juratus* "Sigillum Dei" is visible in the manuscript record.
3. **Scholarly recognition:** Harkness, Szönyi, Klaassen, and Hedegård (editor of the critical *Liber Juratus*) all identify this parallel in print.
4. **Epistemic humility:** the Archive does **not** claim Dee copied the Sigillum from Sloane MS 313. The Archive claims (a) the structural parallel is specific, (b) Dee worked inside a tradition where this sigil type was known, and (c) whether Dee received his Sigillum from the angels, from his reading, from his imagination, or from some interaction of all three is a question the Seeker rank does not resolve.

## III.7 Rule of Use

The **◎** badge appears in-line in lesson prose, immediately after the claim it qualifies. It is never used alone; it is always accompanied by a named parallel source (at minimum) in the Source Pack. The Archive commits that every **◎** deployed in curriculum text will be traceable, through this Source Pack series, to a specific documentary parallel of at least Sloane-MS-313-level specificity.

# §IV — Medieval and Pre-Enochian Precedents

Detailed source entries for the precedents named under the **◎** badge in Lesson 1.3.

## IV.1 *Liber Juratus Honorii* (the *Sworn Book of Honorius*)

**What it is.** A compilation of Christian ritual magic, traditionally attributed to "Honorius of Thebes" but in fact a medieval compilation consolidated in the 13th–14th centuries. The *Liber Juratus* is one of the most important pre-modern Western ritual-magic texts. It includes invocations of angels, elaborate preparatory practices, and a central magical diagram: the **Sigillum Dei** ("Seal of God").

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.**
- Two concentric circles enclosing divine names.
- A heptagonal organizational scheme (often described as "seven heptagons" or a seven-layer arrangement of names).
- A central star figure.
- Extensive use of Hebrew, Latin, and sometimes Greek divine names.
- The logic: a sigil that, when constructed correctly and approached with proper preparation, opens communication with the angelic/divine realm.

**What is genuinely different.**
- The *Liber Juratus* Sigillum Dei is fixed and inherited; Dee's Sigillum Dei Aemeth was "dictated" and corrected session by session, producing a specific version Dee and Kelley understood as given to them.
- The *Liber Juratus* frames its sigil within a more straightforwardly medieval Christian theological architecture; Dee's framing is Renaissance, syncretic, and tied to his own natural-philosophical project.
- The names are not identical. The Sigillum Dei Aemeth contains onomastic material that does not appear in *Liber Juratus* witnesses — names the sessions produced.
- The **functional purpose** differs subtly. In *Liber Juratus* the Sigillum is primarily protective and foundational (one wears or contemplates it for protection and vision). In the Dee–Kelley work, the Sigillum supports the Holy Table — it is architectural, foundational for the whole apparatus.

**Evidence Dee knew the text.** **Strong.** Dee's library (reconstructable via the Roberts & Watson catalogue of his library, 1990) included numerous manuscripts of medieval ritual magic. Klaassen and Láng both argue that the *Liber Juratus* tradition was part of the literate magical culture Dee had absorbed. There is no surviving explicit statement from Dee "I am deriving my Sigillum from *Liber Juratus*," but the tradition was part of his intellectual environment.

**Is the similarity symbolic rather than historical?** The Archive's position is: **both**. There is a real historical-textual substrate — Dee worked inside a tradition where this sigil family existed, and he almost certainly had seen versions of it. There is also a genuinely *new* artifact — the specific Sigillum Dei Aemeth as Dee and Kelley produced it, through the sessions, is not an identical copy of any known Liber Juratus exemplar. The **◎** badge is precisely what holds both of these truths simultaneously.

**Scholarly citation anchor.** Hedegård, Gösta. *Liber iuratus Honorii: A Critical Edition of the Latin Version of the Sworn Book of Honorius* (Stockholm, 2002). Peterson (online at *Esoteric Archives*) also provides accessible transcriptions and images.

## IV.2 Sloane MS 313, British Library

**What it is.** One of the principal English-language manuscript witnesses of the *Liber Juratus* tradition. Dates from the 14th century, with later ownership marks. It survives in the Sloane collection in the British Library.

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.** It contains a *Sigillum Dei* drawing of the specific two-circle, heptagonal, divine-name type that structurally precedes the Sigillum Dei Aemeth.

**What is genuinely different.** See IV.1 above — Sloane MS 313 gives the *medieval* Sigillum Dei, not the 1582-dictated Sigillum Dei Aemeth.

**Evidence Dee knew the text.** **Circumstantial but plausible.** Sloane MS 313 was in English hands in Dee's era. It is not *proven* that Dee held this specific manuscript, but the manuscript is representative of the class of texts Dee is known to have collected. A scholar can point to Sloane MS 313 and say: *this is the kind of manuscript Dee plausibly encountered; the Sigillum tradition reached him through manuscripts like this one.*

**Is the similarity symbolic rather than historical?** For Sloane MS 313 specifically, the similarity is **formal / iconographic**. Whether Dee saw this specific manuscript matters less than the fact that the sigil-type it witnesses was in circulation in the literate magical culture Dee inhabited.

**Scholarly citation anchor.** Klaassen 2013 treats Sloane MS 313 explicitly in the *Liber Juratus* tradition. Hedegård 2002 catalogues it among the witnesses.

## IV.3 Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia libri tres* (1531/1533)

**What it is.** The defining Renaissance systematic work on magic — Christian, Hermetic, Neoplatonic, Kabbalistic, natural-magical. Synthesizes medieval ritual magic with Florentine humanist Hermeticism.

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.** Agrippa provides the theological and cosmological architecture in which the Dee sessions make sense: a ladder of correspondences, angelic hierarchies aligned with planetary and elemental orders, theurgic methods of approach, and the Renaissance assumption that properly prepared ritual communication with celestial intelligences is possible and legitimate.

**What is genuinely different.** Agrippa is a *theorist* and *systematizer*; Dee is a *practitioner* who believed he was receiving a new, specific revelation that extended, corrected, and sometimes superseded prior magical learning.

**Evidence Dee knew the text.** **Overwhelming.** Dee owned Agrippa; scholars have traced specific Agrippan frameworks in Dee's earlier and later work (Clulee 1988; Harkness 1999).

**Type of parallel.** Not a direct sigil parallel; this is a **landscape parallel**. Agrippa defines the landscape in which Dee's sessions are legible. The **◎** here would be invoked around the angelic hierarchies rather than the Sigillum.

## IV.4 Ficino, *De vita libri tres* (1489), Book III

**What it is.** Marsilio Ficino's treatise on the care of the scholar's body, mind, and soul — including methods for drawing down planetary influences through music, image, and contemplative practice.

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.** Book III provides the inner-preparatory logic: the idea that the soul, properly attuned, becomes receptive to higher influence.

**What is genuinely different.** Ficino's "drawing down" is Neoplatonic contemplation; Dee's sessions are dialogic. Ficino's celestial influences are cosmic forces; Dee's voices are agentive entities with names and speech.

**Evidence Dee knew the text.** **Strong.** Dee read the Florentine Neoplatonists.

**Type of parallel.** Background / landscape. Low **◎** intensity for the sessions themselves; higher for Dee's *disposition*.

## IV.5 *1 Enoch* (the Ethiopic Book of Enoch) — the Special Case

**What it is.** An ancient Jewish apocalyptic text (2nd c. BCE onward) describing the antediluvian patriarch Enoch's ascent, his encounters with the Watchers (fallen angels), the seventy shepherds, and an angelology far more elaborate than anything in the Hebrew Bible.

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.**
- Hierarchical angelic orders.
- Named angels acting as pedagogues.
- The structural idea that angelic knowledge is delivered in discrete revelatory units.
- The numeric architecture around sevens and their multiples.
- The *notion* of a language or knowledge associated with pre-diluvian revelation.

**What is genuinely different.** Almost everything at the level of content. *1 Enoch* is apocalyptic-cosmological; Dee's sessions are theurgic-operational. The entities are not the same; the plot is not the same; the purposes are not the same.

**Is there evidence Dee knew *1 Enoch*?** **No — and this is crucial.** *1 Enoch* in its full Ethiopic form was unknown to Western Europe in Dee's lifetime; it was first brought to Europe by James Bruce in the 1770s. Dee knew *the name* Enoch; he knew the brief biblical references ("Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him" — Genesis 5:24); he knew the pseudo-Enochian fragments circulating in late-medieval and Renaissance sources. **He did not know the *Book of Enoch* as it exists in 1 Enoch.**

**Why this matters for the **◎** badge.** The resemblance between the Dee material and *1 Enoch* is a **◎** Parallel of the strongest kind: specific, documentable, **and impossible to explain by direct inheritance**. This is precisely the condition under which **◎** does its most important work. The Archive can say:

> The structural resemblance is real. The historical inheritance is impossible. Whatever the reader makes of that is a choice the Archive leaves to the reader.

This is also why the Archive names the shadow concept *The Language Before Babel* (Cartographer-rank content): because the material of Dee invites — but does not require — the reader to ask whether human beings return, under certain conditions, to patterns older than their texts.

The Archive does not answer that question. It marks it with **◎** and lets it stand.

## IV.6 The Solomonic Tradition (Key of Solomon, Lemegeton, etc.)

**What it is.** A family of late medieval through early modern ritual-magic texts, Christian in framing but drawing on Jewish angelological and demonological material.

**What resembles the Dee/Kelley material.** Angelic invocation; named hierarchies; preparatory disciplines (fasting, purity, specific garments, specific times); sealed materials; the general genre of "communication with higher intelligences via ritual."

**What is genuinely different.** The Solomonic tradition often emphasizes command and coercion; Dee's sessions are overwhelmingly devotional and receptive. The Solomonic tradition often includes demonic material; Dee's early sessions explicitly aim at the angelic.

**Evidence Dee knew the tradition.** **Strong.** Dee collected manuscripts in this tradition.

**Type of parallel.** Genre-level **◎**. Dee's work is legible as a radically devout variant within the broader ritual-magical genre. This does not mean Dee's work *is* that genre — and the **◎** explicitly does not make that claim.

# §VI — The Problem of Single-Witness Testimony

Lesson 1.3 introduces the Seeker's third discernment practice: how to evaluate a testimony that only one person could witness. This section expands the source-level analysis behind that practice.

## VI.1 The Mediation Chain

Every claim in the early Dee–Kelley record is mediated through a chain:

| Layer | Who / what | What they add or filter |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1. Primary witness | Edward Kelley | Vision, hearing, report. No external corroboration for what he saw in the stone. |
| 2. Recorder | John Dee | Transcribes Kelley's words; selects what to write; frames the transcription theologically; occasionally supplies his own inferences. |
| 3. Scribal tradition | Copyists across time (though *Liber Primus* is largely Dee's autograph, later portions have copy layers) | Can introduce scribal error, selection, or annotation. For *Liber Primus* this is minimal. |
| 4. Editor | Casaubon (1659); later Peterson (2003), Harkness (1999), Bassnett (2008) | Each editor's framing shapes what the modern reader encounters. Casaubon hostile; Peterson literal/preservationist; Harkness critical-historical; Bassnett biographical. |
| 5. Modern scholar | Contemporary academic commentators | Interpretive frameworks, disciplinary assumptions, and historical distance. |
| 6. Modern practitioner | Occult and magical practitioners who use the material | Operational frameworks that re-contextualize the material for present-day practice. |
| 7. The reader of the Archive | You | Your own assumptions, beliefs, and interpretive dispositions. |

**The implication.** Every line you read in Lesson 1.3 has passed through between five and seven of these layers. The Archive's discipline is to **mark each layer honestly** and never pretend the reader has direct access to the original event. There is no direct access. There is only the chain — and the integrity of the chain is what the Archive protects.

## VI.2 Primary vs. Recorder: the Kelley-Dee Problem

At the primary level, the problem is structural: **Kelley alone could see.** Dee, who was the trained mathematician, natural philosopher, and theologian, could not verify independently. His relationship to the visions was entirely mediated through Kelley's report.

This gives the Kelley–Dee pair a specific epistemic shape:
- Kelley's report is constitutive of the event (no Kelley, no vision).
- Dee's record is constitutive of the archive (no Dee, no transmission to us).
- **Dee trusted Kelley more than Kelley's own historical reputation would warrant**, because Dee's theological and procedural structure provided discernment criteria that *he* applied to *Kelley's* material (prayer, coherence with scripture, fruitfulness, alignment with previous sessions).

## VI.3 The Recorder as Verifier

Dee was not a passive transcriber. He was, within his own epistemology, the verifier. He tested Kelley's reports against:
- theological orthodoxy (did the entity say anything heretical?)
- internal consistency (did later sessions contradict earlier ones?)
- Dee's own contemplative and prayer life (did the material feel right in prayer?)
- the material's fruitfulness (did following it produce meaningful results?)

When Dee did eventually doubt — and he did, notoriously, several times — he recorded that doubt. The *Liber Primus* includes moments where Dee is troubled, skeptical, or in spiritual distress about what is being transmitted.

**For Seeker rank: this is the key shape.** Dee is not naive. Dee is *disciplined*. The discernment practice in Lesson 1.3 is patterned on Dee's own.

## VI.4 Editor as Frame

The shift from manuscript to print, via Casaubon in 1659, is the moment the Dee material entered public reception — and it entered it in a hostile frame. Casaubon's preface treats Dee as a pious man deceived by demons. Every reader from 1659 forward who met the material through Casaubon met it already in that frame.

Peterson's 2003 edition, by contrast, is preservationist and neutral. Harkness's 1999 monograph places the material within Dee's natural-philosophical project and refuses the "deceived by demons" frame.

**This is why the Archive cites Peterson and Harkness rather than Casaubon for substantive claims,** even while acknowledging Casaubon's historical role.

## VI.5 The Modern Scholar and the Archive

Modern scholarship is itself a mediating layer. The Archive is explicit about this: we are not reporting from the primary record directly; we are reporting from the primary record *as read by* Harkness, Peterson, Asprem, Bassnett, Clulee, Szönyi, and Klaassen.

This is not a weakness. It is the condition of every historical archive. What the Archive commits to is *naming the mediation* rather than hiding it.

# §VII — The First Names

Detailed source entries for the earliest angelic names introduced in Lesson 1.3.

## VII.1 Annael

**Source reference.** Sloane MS 3188, *Liber Primus*, early March 1582 sessions. Peterson 2003 provides the critical edition.

**Historical precedent.** The name **Anael / Anahel / Annael** appears in medieval Jewish and Christian angelologies, particularly in connection with the sphere of Venus. Agrippa's *De occulta philosophia* Book III names Anael among the seven planetary archangels. The name appears in the Solomonic tradition, in various Hekhalot fragments that had reached medieval Europe in Latin excerpts, and in *Liber Juratus* witnesses.

**Was it already known to Dee?** **Almost certainly yes.** Dee had read Agrippa. Anael was not an unfamiliar or shocking name in Dee's milieu.

**Is its appearance surprising or expected?** **Expected.** That the first angelic entity to name itself uses a name already deeply embedded in Dee's magical-theological vocabulary is, from the Historical Lens, unsurprising. From the Traditional Occult Lens, it confirms that the communication is happening within a recognized angelic order. From the Psychological Lens, it is evidence that the co-constructed field of the sessions naturally drew on vocabulary both men already shared. These three readings do not contradict each other — they attribute different causes to the same observed fact.

**Badge.** **◆** for the record; **◎** for the precedent; **?** for the nature of the entity.

## VII.2 Uriel

**Source reference.** Sloane MS 3188, *Liber Primus*, March 1582 onward. Peterson 2003.

**Historical precedent.** **Deep.** Uriel appears in:
- *2 Esdras* (4 Ezra) — canonical in the Vulgate as an apocryphal book Dee would have known
- Jewish and Christian angelological literature
- Medieval ritual-magic texts (including *Liber Juratus*)
- Agrippa
- Pseudo-Dionysius via medieval reception
- Popular devotional literature

**Was it already known to Dee?** **Unquestionably.**

**Is its appearance surprising or expected?** **Fully expected.** Uriel was among the most recognizable archangelic names available to a late-16th-century English Christian Hermeticist.

**Badge.** **◆** / **◎**.

## VII.3 Michael

**Source reference.** Sloane MS 3188, *Liber Primus*. Peterson 2003.

**Historical precedent.** **Canonical.** Michael appears in the Book of Daniel, Jude, Revelation, in all major Jewish and Christian angelologies, and in virtually every Western magical tradition.

**Was it already known to Dee?** **Of course.**

**Is its appearance surprising or expected?** **Fully expected.** Michael as archangel was as familiar as any theological name in Dee's world.

**Badge.** **◆** / **◎**.

## VII.4 Interpretive note on the first names

The fact that the **first** named entities in the Dee–Kelley sessions are names already central to Dee's theological and magical vocabulary is itself a finding worth flagging. It is consistent with:
- the **◇** Historical / Psychological reading: the co-constructed field naturally produced the names its participants brought to it
- the **○** Traditional Occult reading: the communication began with entities already known to the tradition, as one would expect
- the **~** Speculative reading: the entities identified themselves by names the participants would recognize, as a pedagogical accommodation

The Archive, at Seeker rank, presents the three readings without ranking them.

## VII.5 Names held for later ranks

Several names that appear later in the Dee record — including **Madimi** (who appears in 1583 and becomes central to the Bohemian sessions), **Nalvage**, **Gabriel**, and the entities named in the Calls — are held for later lessons. Lesson 1.3 stops at the first wave to preserve the sense of a system just beginning.

## VIII.1 Crystal Shew-Stone — SLCups.232

**BM reference.** Museum number **SLCups.232**. Sloane Cups catalogue. Held within the British Museum John Dee object group.

**Role in Lesson 1.3.** The visual focus of the sessions; the object at the center of the Holy Table into which Kelley looked.

**Historically certain.** That this is a rock crystal sphere; that it entered the British Museum via the Sloane bequest provenance stream; that it is traditionally identified with Dee's early sessions. ◆ High.

**Historically uncertain / added by tradition.** That this exact stone was used in *any specific named session* is not provable. Dee used multiple shew-stones across his career. The identification of SLCups.232 with the earliest Mortlake transmissions is **traditional and plausible**, not documentarily guaranteed. ○ High for the tradition; **?** for which exact session.

**Lesson-level framing.** Lesson 1.3 handles this honestly: it names SLCups.232, names the John Dee object group, and notes that unbroken provenance from Dee's hand is not established.

## VIII.2 Obsidian Mirror — 1966,1001.1

**BM reference.** Registration **1966,1001.1**. With Horace Walpole's case.

**Role in Lesson 1.3.** Mentioned only as part of the broader artifact set. Not load-bearing.

**Historically certain.** Mesoamerican (Aztec/Mixtec) obsidian mirror. The object itself is unambiguous. ◆ High.

**Later tradition added.** The identification of this specific mirror with John Dee is Horace Walpole's (18th-century). Whether Dee actually used this mirror is **not established**. ? Contested.

**Lesson-level framing.** Lesson 1.3 does not assert Dee used this mirror; it notes the object and flags the later attribution.

## VIII.3 Gold Disc ("Vision of the Four Castles") — 1942,0506.1

**BM reference.** Registration **1942,0506.1**.

**Role in Lesson 1.3.** Background artifact; mentioned for completeness.

**Historically certain.** The disc exists and is engraved with the design traditionally identified with a Dee-session vision. ◆ High for the object.

**Later tradition.** The identification with the specific vision is traditional; the object itself predates modern cataloguing. ○ Moderate-to-High.

## VIII.4 Sigillum Dei Aemeth and Wax Discs — 1838,1232.90

**BM reference.** Registration **1838,1232.90** with associated letter suffixes for the four supporting discs. Sir Hans Sloane bequest provenance.

**Role in Lesson 1.3.** Central. The dictated design of the Sigillum is one of the defining events of the first transmissions. The wax discs are the principal material witness to that design.

**Historically certain.** The wax discs exist; they are inscribed with the design described in *Liber Primus* and the subsequent books; the design matches Dee's manuscript drawings; the object entered the Sloane bequest with an unusually strong provenance. ◆ High.

**Scholarly consensus.** These are the discs Dee and Kelley produced. ◇ High.

**The ◎ anchor.** The Sigillum's structural resemblance to the *Liber Juratus* Sigillum Dei is the inaugural **◎** Parallel claim (§III.6, §IV.1, §IV.2).
